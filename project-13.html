Aesthetics of Failure

01 · Project Concept

When the system refuses precision

This project centers on the aesthetics of failure, using gesture interaction to drive visual feedback and explore how recognition errors in human-computer interaction can be transformed into an expressive visual language. When the system misinterprets hand gestures, the visuals display “glitch” effects such as pixel distortion, image displacement, and repetitive overlays. These outputs—traditionally regarded as “errors”—are reinterpreted in this project as an artistic aesthetic arising from the inherent uncertainty within technical processes, thereby questioning the implicit reliance on precision and total control in digital interaction.

Failure is not removed from the interaction. It becomes the interaction.

Relevant Background Information

01 · System Norm

Precision as the Default

This project sits at the intersection of interaction design, machine perception, and glitch aesthetics. Conventional human-computer interaction prioritises precision, efficiency, and control, positioning error as technical failure. Digital art and critical design challenge this norm by treating system failure, inaccurate recognition, and unpredictable outcomes as generative material.

  • Precision
  • Efficiency
  • Control
02 · Critical Lens

Failure as Creative Material

The aesthetics of failure reveals creative potential in technical limitation and breakdown. Rather than correcting misrecognition in computer-vision interfaces, this project preserves it as material for aesthetic exploration and critical reflection on trust, embodiment, and authority in digital environments.

  • Trust
  • Embodiment
  • Authority
03 · Project Position

Gesture into Glitch

Technically, the project employs real-time gesture tracking to drive visual outputs. When the system misinterprets input, it produces glitch-like effects—such as pixel fragmentation, displacement, or repetition—which are deliberately preserved and amplified to challenge the dominance of seamless interaction design.

  • Fragmentation
  • Displacement
  • Repetition

04 · Visual Research

Building a vocabulary from glitch

Early research examined recurring features of glitch art—not as a style to copy, but as a set of behaviours that could emerge from live interaction and changing system values.

What is Glitch Art?

Glitch art uses errors, malfunctions, and unintended artefacts in analogue or digital systems as aesthetic material. Broken signals, corrupted data, compression artefacts, and displaced pixels are not concealed; they are selected, produced, or amplified to reveal the technologies that normally remain invisible behind a seamless image.

1960s–70s

Electronic disruption

Early video artists manipulated television signals and hardware. Works such as Nam June Paik’s Magnet TV and TV Cello made electronic interference visible as an artistic event.

1990s–2000s

Digital error becomes a medium

Artists began working with software crashes, corrupted files, compression errors, and databending, turning the material limits of digital media into recognisable visual forms.

2010s–Now

From effect to critique

Practitioners and theorists including Rosa Menkman framed glitch as more than a visual style: it can expose technological norms, interrupt expectations, and question systems built around control.

01

Resolution breaks down and the digital image reveals its smallest units.

Noise & Grain

03

Colour Degradation

Colour and luminance collapse into abrupt tonal bands, replacing continuous transitions with visible steps.

05 · Interaction Design

From gesture to unstable image

The system moves through four linked stages, from tracking a moving hand to reframing the resulting visual interruption.

  1. 01

    Sense

    A webcam captures the moving hand and supplies the live image used for landmark tracking.

  2. 02

    Interpret

    MediaPipe reads hand landmarks and converts changing positions and distances into control values.

  3. 03

    Misinterpret

    Variations in recognition and mapping trigger pixel distortion, image displacement, noise, and repetitive overlays.

  4. 04

    Reframe

    Outputs multiply, disappear, repeat, and shift position, turning a technical error into an expressive visual event.

Gesture State · A

Open Hand

An open palm exposes and expands the image field, allowing more fragments and transformations to become visible.

Gesture State · B

Clenched Fist

Closing the hand compresses the field and intensifies the sense of interruption, accumulation, and unstable feedback.

Spatial Mapping · Six Regions

The screen becomes an instrument

Hand position is interpreted across six screen regions. Moving between them selects different processing conditions, while the gesture state changes their intensity. The interaction is simple to learn, but never produces exactly the same image twice.

06 · Technical Development

A deliberately unstable instrument

The prototype combines webcam input, hand-landmark tracking, and real-time image processing. The technical system is used as a research instrument: its uncertainty is exposed and shaped instead of hidden behind seamless interaction.

Webcam-based hand movement
Tracking

Visual System

Methods

Software Roles

Tracking and image-making operate as one feedback loop.

MediaPipe supplies the changing gesture data; TouchDesigner turns that data into a real-time visual response. The project depends on the gap between these two layers rather than treating either tool as a perfectly transparent interface.

01 · Input and Recognition

MediaPipe

MediaPipe is used for real-time hand-landmark tracking. It analyses the webcam image to locate points on the moving hand; their coordinates and changing distances provide the gesture data passed into the visual system.

  • Webcam image input
  • Hand-landmark detection
  • Position and distance values
02 · Processing and Feedback

TouchDesigner

TouchDesigner receives the mapped tracking values and processes the imagery in real time. Pixelation, displacement, edge processing, noise, and repeated tiles are combined into a visual composition that changes as the hand moves.

  • Real-time value mapping
  • Image processing and compositing
  • Responsive tiled output

Inside the Source File

Gesture data becomes spatial composition.

The TouchDesigner project contains Python DATs that measure hand landmarks and reorganise the output containers. Distance and rotation values describe the moving hand; gesture counts then determine how many image regions appear and where they are placed.

08 · Reflection

Error as a collaborator

The experiment reframes recognition error as a form of visual authorship. Neither the participant nor the software controls the final image alone; the work emerges through negotiation between gesture, tracking, mapping, and system behaviour.

Future iterations could isolate different kinds of error, compare stable and unstable mappings, and document how participants interpret moments when the system does not respond as expected.

The process also showed that variation needs to remain legible. If every effect is active at once, complexity becomes undifferentiated noise; clearer transitions between gesture states would give future participants more agency while preserving the work’s unpredictability.
